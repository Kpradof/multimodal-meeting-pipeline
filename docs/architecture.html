Multimodal pipeline · AMI Meeting Corpus

Channels that contradict

A meeting leaves three trails: what was said, what was projected, and what was drawn. A pipeline that listens to only one of them has no way of knowing when it is wrong. This one crosses them.

01

The architecture

The first three columns are the classic pipeline: every modality becomes text that lands in a single vector table, with audio and video carrying timestamps and the documents carrying none. The fourth column is what changes the outcome, a second consumer that is not looking for information but for disagreements.

02

Why the second output is needed

The AMI corpus hands every team the same finance slide. In meeting ES2008a, two of its figures ended up recorded wrong, and not for the same reason.

Channel Profit aim Maximum cost
Projected slide 50 M euro 12.50 euro
Minutes written by the PM 50 M euro 12.50 euro
AMI manual transcript fifteen million twelve fifty
AMI official answer key fifteen million 12.50 euro
Whisper large-v3-turbo 50 million 1250 euro

Two figures, one slide. The human annotator got the first wrong. The model got the second wrong. Neither could have known: both were only listening.

The meeting also carries its own arithmetic proof. The speaker says, and both transcribe it correctly: “we're selling it for twice what we'd like to produce it for”. Twenty five is twice 12.50. Not twice 1250.

This is not a one meeting anecdote. Of the 142 hand-annotated summaries in the corpus, twelve record a profit aim: ten say 50 M, two say 15 M, for a figure that was projected on screen in front of the annotator.

03

One pipeline, two engines

Every stage is an interface with two implementations. local runs free on any machine; snowflake uses Cortex. The embedding model is literally the same on both sides, so the vectors are comparable, and that is a test rather than a README promise.

Stage Snowflake Cortex Local
Transcribe AI_TRANSCRIBE whisper large-v3-turbo
Read documents AI_PARSE_DOCUMENT direct extraction + Claude vision
Chunk SPLIT_TEXT_RECURSIVE_CHARACTER segment aligned chunking
Embed AI_EMBED the same arctic-embed-m
Search VECTOR_COSINE_SIMILARITY array_cosine_similarity
Count tokens AI_COUNT_TOKENS messages.count_tokens()
Generate CORTEX.COMPLETE Claude API
Why two

A repo that requires a paid Snowflake account is one almost nobody runs. One that never mentions it does not demonstrate the stack. The abstraction solves both.

What it teaches

Writing the interface forces you to understand what each Cortex function does, not just copy the call.

What is missing

The Snowflake engine is not written. A trial account blocks every AI function, so it could not be verified today.

04

What it cost to run

All of the above, end to end, over public data, on a laptop.

17× real time, ASR
133 minutes transcribed
574 chunks indexed
$0.07 API cost per run

Transcription, embeddings, vector search and token counting cost nothing: they run locally on Apple Silicon, or they are calls that are not billed. The only spend was describing 15 whiteboard frames.

05

Limits, said out loud

  • The overhead camera was dropped from the index. Its 17 frames returned nearly the same sentence, a static room with an oval table, so it spent tokens and polluted the ranking. The whiteboard does add signal: the strokes evolve between frames.
  • The generated channel has to speak the language of the corpus. With the vision prompt in Spanish over an English corpus, the descriptions formed their own vector neighborhood: language outweighed topic. A Spanish question about pricing retrieved ten of ten video frames, none about pricing.
  • Scene detection fails on whiteboards. It works on slide decks, which cut hard. Strokes accumulate slowly: at a threshold of 0.02, ffmpeg returns zero frames. That channel needs interval sampling.
  • Document extraction leaked binary debris. Fragments like <real>774</real> were taking real slots in the top ten. A stricter prose filter cut the document channel from 621 lines to 322 and the index from 873 to 574 chunks. Fewer rows, better ranking.
  • The similarity threshold belongs to the model. arctic-embed-m rarely passes 0.4 on this corpus. A 0.5 threshold returns zero rows without raising anything.
  • A meeting without documents has nothing to check itself against. ES2008d publishes none, so only audio exists there.
